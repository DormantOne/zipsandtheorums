What the program actually proves

  • Interactive authentication: independent verifier challenges are chosen after each commitment.
  • Mutual: Alice verifies Bob and Bob verifies Alice, every time.
  • Freshness: two new 128-bit nonces, an exact step number and previous transcript digest are bound into the commitments.
  • Repetition: the initiator alternates and every new exchange is chained to its predecessor.
  • Assumption: each party already knows the other’s authentic public-key fingerprint.

What it does not prove

  • Not encryption: exchanged messages are public; no shared secret is produced.
  • Not a transferable signature: this version deliberately uses a live zero-knowledge-style identification protocol.
  • Not proven secure: a 1,000-square secret has no audited classical or quantum security estimate.
  • Not quantum-certified: increasing puzzle size and round count cannot establish resistance to quantum algorithms.
  • Not Internet-ready: both identities are simulated locally; no network transport or public-key distribution.
